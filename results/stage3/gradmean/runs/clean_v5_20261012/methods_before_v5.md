## Estimation and uncertainty

For K batches, S=sum(s_b) and Q=sum(||s_b||²), estimate noise trace as `(Q−||S||²/K)/(K−1)` and squared mean as `(||S||²−Q)/(K*(K−1))`. Cross-estimator means subtract same-batch scalar products before division byK(K−1). Different-point data are independent; use products of their sample means. Multiply noise trace by80 for the prescribed equivalent per-episode scale. B_ep is this trace divided by squared mean; it remains a batch-equivalent scale for LOO-coupled estimators.

Twenty dedicated random balanced splits of groups produce mean split cosine rho. Report `sqrt(2*rho/(1+rho))` only when rho>0; otherwise report N/A (direction not resolved). The moment-based direction check is N/A when the squared-signal estimate is nonpositive. Neither unavailable value stops collection. If the squared-signal estimate is nonpositive or its90% interval includes zero, report B_ep as the lower bound `80*trace/upper(signal interval)` and derive the K_needed lower bound from it. A nonpositive or unavailable upper limit gives no finite bound; the point remains open. Keep signed signal and out-of-range cosine estimates rather than clipping. Identical split randomization is reused across delete-one-group replicates, with balance restored after deletion, so computational randomness is controlled.

The approved covariance effective-rank denominator uses X_g=S_g/50 and the mean over distinct groups a,b,c,d of `50²*((X_a−X_b)·(X_c−X_d))²/4`. It cancels the unknown mean. The original raw-inner-product variance additionally contains100*uᵀΣ_b*u. The rank estimate is `(trΣ_b)²/tr(Σ_b²)` and, like other ratios, has finite-sample uncertainty.

Use delete-one-group jackknife standard errors and90% intervals `estimate±1.645*SE`, paired across variants at one point and independently over each point for cross-point comparisons. Ratio intervals are unavailable if required deletion replicates are undefined. One pilot group cannot provide group-jackknife uncertainty. Ordinary jackknife can overcover for nearly degenerate quadratic signal estimates; the synthetic gate tests the specified setup and is not a universal coverage guarantee.

The first collect job at each point samples200 decisions with a dedicated generator, storing the squared score norm and decision index. Report the mean, maximum k-decile mean and99th percentile; group-jackknife uncertainty is unavailable because all scores are in one group. The reward maximum and score moments give an empirical plug-in variance-lemma comparison, not a proven reward/score bound.

## Addendum G2: paper estimator with the frozen critic

V6 (`theory_critic`) compares the variance of the same discounted-objective score estimator with a different baseline. Its per-decision coefficient is `gamma**k * (G - c*V)`, where G is raw-reward Monte Carlo return cut at the 250-decision episode boundary, V is the prepared frozen critic, and `c = sqrt(ret_rms.var + epsilon)` is its frozen reward-scaler divisor. The score sums 24 coordinates and 10 fine-tuned transitions, with unit denoising weights, no advantage normalization and no log-probability clamp. The code ascent gradient is multiplied by2,500 for paper units; squared quantities use2,500². No actor, critic or scaler update is permitted during collection.

The `gm_include_critic` flag defaults to false and is passed as `+train.gm_include_critic=true` only to newly launched collection jobs after H1–H4 pass. V1–V5 remain measured on every batch. Existing jobs and finished evidence are unchanged. The user replaced adaptive continuation before any V6 data for compute reasons. Existing running jobs finish and count, while new legacy admissions stop. Then exactly ten new six-variant groups (500 batches) per policy run in P0, D2, N2, D1, N1 order, and collection stops. These groups also enter V1–V5 estimates. Earlier closure records remain historical evidence; no further correlation-based stopping or extra rounds apply.

For V6 covariance trace and its own squared signal, use the same whole-batch pairwise estimators as AddendumG, on six-variant groups only. Compare V6/V4 and V5/V6 covariance traces on that same matched cohort. For V6 critical batch size, divide V6's episode-equivalent trace by V4's squared-signal estimate from all available groups. Joint group deletion removes a six-variant group from both quantities or a legacy group only from the denominator. Independent legacy/new cohorts contribute their delete-group jackknife variances; intervals are estimate±1.645SE. Nonpositive or unresolved signal is reported as unavailable or the existing upper-signal lower bound, never repaired by clipping.

The V6/V4 mean-direction cosine uses both estimators on the six-variant cohort and excludes same-batch products. At scheduled analysis boundaries, a valid90% interval wholly below1, with both squared signals resolved positive, is a consistency failure requiring a stop and report. An unresolved cosine remains inconclusive. Repeated diagnostic checks do not provide a globally calibrated hypothesis test.

Within each of five50-decision bins, report the episode-mean sum of `gamma**(2*k) * (G-baseline)**2` for each baseline, their ratio, and the mean residual. Baseline ratios are ratios of pooled energies, with paired group uncertainty. Interpretations concern these five fixed policies and their prepared critics, not a population of independently trained policy paths.

Verification is CPU-only: explicit float64 coefficients(H1), LOO and zero substitutions through the same V6 gradient path(H2), two fresh batches from each old/new collector with exact V1–V5 scientific scalars, group sums and global RNG states(H3), and exact prepared frozen-state loading(H4). Wall-clock measurements are excluded from replay equality. The gate has a two-hour cap on 40 CPUs / 64 GiB and no GPU. The fixed collection shares the existing 125 GPU-hour campaign cap, with one-hour per-job caps and at most five GPU workers while E1C has priority. Expected remaining collection time is about 2.1 hours: 50 groups × 725 seconds / five workers. Scheduled campaign and worker monitoring runs every 30 minutes, following max(30 minutes, expected duration / 6).
